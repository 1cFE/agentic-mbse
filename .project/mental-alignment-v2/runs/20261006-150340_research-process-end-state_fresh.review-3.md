## Findings

1. The decode-the-figure facts for the two smaller diagrams live in captions only. "the first 'yes' wins" (close diagram, section "The rules", figcaption) and "checks in order, every exit a receipt" (register diagram, same section) are what let a reader read the diagram, and neither appears in body text next to the figure. The only fuller descriptions are the `.sr` paragraphs, which are visually hidden. The rules list sits above the diagrams, but the figures have no reading guide saying what to look at first, what the yes/no arrows mean, or what the right-hand column is. (cites: Self-contained visuals, "explain its parts and reading order in nearby body text rather than hiding essential meaning in a caption"; feedback "Caption carrying the fact that decodes the figure")

2. The big overview diagram (section "The picture") has no reading guide for sighted readers either. Its caption is one sentence ("Time runs left to right in each half") plus the example ID. Its lane, wall, and arrow structure is explained only in the hidden `.sr` text, and the "Terms" paragraph comes after the diagram although the diagram labels already use "receipt", "trail", "hold-out", and "seam". (cites: Self-contained visuals; Clear on one reading, "define unfamiliar terms"; feedback "Caption carrying the fact that decodes the figure")

3. The big diagram's text is small at normal desktop widths. It is a 1480-unit-wide SVG inside a 1180px main column (padding included, about 1130px of room), so it scales to roughly 0.76. The 11.5px and 12.5px labels (`.hdk`, `.mono`, `.bxs`) render near 9 to 10px, and the 13.5px box titles near 10px. Below 1000px it switches to a fixed 1480px width with horizontal scroll, so the whole model is never visible at once on a phone or narrow window. (cites: Self-contained visuals, "readable labels"; Hard constraints, "readable contrast")

4. The concrete thread is thin. REQ-036-01 (cryogenic 316LN allowable stress) appears only inside the first diagram's labels and the JSON appendix. The summary, the rules, and the other two diagrams never use it. The diagram traces only the happy path (registered, "≤ 2 captures"). The refusal, duplicate, and negative paths that the later diagrams and rules 3, 4, and 6 describe are never walked through the same request. The example is introduced by a caption line, not by prose after the high-level model. (cites: A concrete thread, "Carry the synthesis's strongest example through the page, or place it immediately after the high-level model")

5. Section references are not links, and one points at nothing. The summary uses "four classes", "the wall", "receipts" and `DI-XXX` with no link to where each is broken down; the only links on the page are the top nav. Captions say "Rules 2 and 3" and "Rules 1, 3, 4 and 5" as bare numbers over an ordered list with no anchors. The port table (appendix) says "decision 1" in the `research-acquire` row, but the "Decisions needed" items are an unnumbered bullet list, so "decision 1" has no visible referent. (cites: A connected page, "working references to later sections"; feedback "Concept left for the reader to scroll for", "Make every section reference a working link. Do not write a bare section number in prose")

6. Several terms are used without ever being defined on the page. "keeper" and "triage" (close diagram, receipt JSON), "limits 5/2" and "consumer WI-036" (first diagram and caption), "run dir", "PREREQUISITE" and "task outcome" (rule 8), "request key (question, consumer, gap type, venues; not limits)" (rule 6), "goal layer" (rule 8), and "approval gate" (rule 7). The Terms paragraph covers rounds, trail, seam, receipt, negative, registry, manifest, and hold-out, but not these. Rules 6 and 8 in particular are unreadable without them. (cites: Clear on one reading, "Assume no source context, define unfamiliar terms, and make every heading and sentence understandable without rereading")

7. The "Terms" block is one dense paragraph of eight definitions, each with nested parentheticals and code identifiers, placed after the figure that depends on it. It reads as a wall of text in a terminal-width column. (cites: Clear on one reading; Aggressive subtraction, "Once a point has landed, stop"; feedback "Concept left for the reader to scroll for", for the ordering problem)

8. The four classes are shown as decision conditions but not as outcomes. The close diagram says how each class is chosen. The body does not say what REGISTERED, OPERATOR_QUEUE, BLOCKER, and BOUNDED_NEGATIVE each mean to the caller or what the goal round does next. The first diagram's "class → trail: task return" does not show a mapping. Only REGISTERED gets a gloss, and that is inside the appendix JSON ("at least one source is registered and citable"). (cites: Concrete detail, "a flow gets its steps, labels, states, and outcomes")

9. Current versus new is not encoded in the visuals. The summary says reading exists today and acquisition is ported in from fusion-tea, but the overview diagram shows both halves in the same style, with no mark for what already ships in agentic-mbse and what is new. The legend defines human, agent, script, and file, and a `--new` color exists in the CSS, but the diagram does not use it for this. The "What the port changes" section is text and a hidden-in-appendix table only. (cites: Faithful distinctions, "distinguish current behavior, intended design…"; Self-contained visuals, "use the same visual encoding consistently")

10. The open decisions are the last thing on the page and absent from the summary. A reader who stops after the summary does not learn that three decisions (separate command or mode, fate of `/manage-sources`, delegating insight approval) are open, nor that the third is unresolved ("Open."). The summary also does not state what problem the wall prevents or why a reader should care. Rule 1's "a fetched page proves reachability, never a fact" comes closest, and it is rule 1 of eight. (cites: Important stuff up front, "If they stop at any point, they should already have consumed the most important information"; Reading experience, "Make the answer and reason to care visible early")

11. Adjacent sections have no transitions. "The picture" opens straight into a figure, "The rules" into a list, "What the port changes" into a bullet list, and "Decisions needed" into bullets, each with no sentence saying why it follows or what the previous section left open. The page is a sequence of well-structured blocks, not a narrated one. (cites: Real transitions, "Layout cannot substitute for a narrative connection between adjacent sections")

12. Some rules are long compound sentences. Rule 2 ("a `registered` receipt makes the class `REGISTERED` whatever the agent says, and a claim with no receipt counts for nothing"), rule 3, and rule 6 each carry two or three ideas joined by semicolons or "and". One of them (rule 6) packs a nested parenthetical list. (cites: Clear on one reading; Aggressive subtraction)

13. The first dropdown's summary line is a noun phrase with a parenthetical, "Receipt, return.json and manifest row (JSON shapes)". It is a list plus a clarifier, not a bare noun phrase. The second ("Port table, file by file") is fine. (cites: feedback "Dropdown summary line written as a double clause")

14. The page labels itself "checkpoint" and shows the question and date, but it has no section named "Judgment". The "Summary" section may be that content, but a page reader cannot tell. This reviewer cannot read the synthesis, so this is unverified. If the synthesis's `# Judgment` is the Summary, the page and the shape agree. (cites: Output shape, "Checkpoint: Include the question and date from metadata plus the synthesis's `# Judgment` section"; feedback "Structural heading turned into a claim", which keeps `Judgment` as a plain name)
